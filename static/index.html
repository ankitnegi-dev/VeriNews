Method

How it works

Four steps from a free-text claim to a verdict, with the evidence kept visible.

  1. 01

    Find coverage

    Search cached or live news for the claim's key people, places, and numbers.

  2. 02

    Rank the evidence

    Order articles by relevance and recency, then weight them by source credibility.

  3. 03

    Read each article

    Score each article as supports, refutes, or neutral against the claim.

  4. 04

    Decide or refuse

    Require at least two relevant articles for SUPPORTED and at least three for REFUTED. Otherwise return not enough evidence.

A verdict needs at least two relevant articles that agree for SUPPORTED and at least three for REFUTED. One article never decides. Conflict gives not enough evidence.

Verdicts

What the verdicts mean

Supported

The evidence points toward the claim.

At least two relevant sources agree, and the weighted support clears the decision gates.

Refuted

The evidence points against the claim.

At least three relevant sources disagree, and the weighted refute score clears the decision gates.

Not enough evidence

The evidence is not strong enough to decide.

The system abstains when evidence is missing, weak, conflicting, or too one-sided.

Trust

Trust and limits

No guessing

A single article never decides. Weak, missing, or conflicting evidence returns not enough evidence.

Open evidence

Every result lists the articles, relevance scores, stance, and source credibility tier.

Visible weights

The verdict shows the support and refute weights that drove the decision.

Honest limits

English only, a small hand-made source table, strict NLI, and a cache that cannot see newer stories.

Claims move fast. Evidence takes longer. Someone may already have reported it.

FAQ

Questions

What does "not enough evidence" mean?

The system could not find at least two relevant articles that agreed. The evidence may be missing, weak, conflicting, or too one-sided. It returns this verdict instead of guessing.

Why is the demo offline?

The demo runs in cache-only mode. It reads stored retrieval and NLI caches and does not call a live news provider. It cannot detect stories newer than the cache.

Which sources count more?

Source credibility is a hand-made table. Stronger sources get more weight, and unknown domains default to low. The table is small, so it is a policy choice, not a fitted model.

What can it get wrong?

It can be strict on multi-fact and numeric claims, confuse different events of the same type, keep previews or live blogs, and let some off-topic evidence through. It is English only and the test set is small. It is a prototype, not a replacement for human fact-checking.
